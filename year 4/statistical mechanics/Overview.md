
Statistical mechanics is the practice of bridging the microscopic to the macroscopic. We aim to study the collective behaviour of a macroscopic system in terms of the dynamics of their microscopic constituents. Specifically, we tackle systems with large DOF with statistical methods.

The microstate is defined as the full specification of the internal DOF of microscopic constituents of a system. Take for example a group of water molecules: The $\mu$-state must specify the coordinates $\underline{x}_i$ as well as the momentum $\dot{\underline{x}}_i$ of each molecule. We then have:$$\Huge \underline{F}_i=m \frac{d^2\underline{x}_i}{dt^2}\implies\text{system of DOF coupled ODEs}$$
The macrostate is defined as the specification of the large object in terms of a small number of quantities. For our water example, we could specify the macrostate with just temperature and volume. Many microstates map to a single macrostate.

We start from:
> Postulates for probability distributions of systems with $N>>1$ constituents
> Microscopic properties and dynamics of constituents

And through our process of statistical mechanics, we infer macroscopic quantities and relations between them. Note that we can do the reverse and infer the existence of microscopic constituents from macroscopic behaviour.
