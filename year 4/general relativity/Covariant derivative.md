
The coordinate derivatives of a vector field do not form a tensor field, under coordinate transformation:$$\Large\partial_\mu V^\mu=\frac{\partial \tilde x^\rho}{\partial x^\mu}\frac{\partial }{\partial \tilde x^\rho}\left(\frac{\partial x^\nu}{\partial \tilde x^\sigma}\tilde V^\sigma\right)=\frac{\partial \tilde x^\rho}{\partial x^\mu}\frac{\partial x^\nu}{\partial \tilde x^\sigma}\tilde\partial_\rho\tilde V^\sigma+\frac{\partial \tilde x^\rho}{\partial x^\mu}\frac{\partial^2x^\nu}{\partial \tilde x^\rho\partial\tilde x^\sigma}\tilde V^\sigma$$Note the second term vanishes for linear coordinate transformations, however this does not yield the expected tensor transformation for more general transformations. There is no coordiante-independent notion of a vector field $V$ being constant. 

Therefore we proceed by introducing the covariant derivative $\nabla$, defined as some linear operation mapping vectors to $[1,1]$ tensors that acts like a derivative:
> The covariant derivative of a vector field $V^\mu$ is defined as a $[1,1]$ tensor field $(\nabla V)^\nu_\mu$, which we write as $\nabla_\mu V^\nu$.
> $\nabla$ is linear, $\nabla(V+W)=\nabla V+\nabla W$.
> $\nabla$ should satisfy the [[Leibniz and Chain rules|Leibniz]] rule $\nabla fV=dfV+f\nabla V$ for a vector field $V$ and function $f$. In component form:$$\Large(\nabla(fV))_\mu^\nu=(\partial_\mu f)V^\nu+f(\nabla V)_\mu^\nu$$

This gives us a coordinate-independent notion of a relation between vectors at different points, i.e. in different tangent spaces. We want to characterise the most general structure satisfying these properties, which we will do by looking at it's action on the basis vectors. We define the connection coefficients by the components of the $[1,1]$ tensor obtained as the covariant derivatives of the coordinate basis vectors:$$\huge\Gamma^\nu_{\mu\lambda}=(\nabla(\partial_\lambda))_\mu^\nu$$This allows us to write the components of the covariant derivative as:$$\Large(\nabla V)_\mu^\nu=(\nabla(V^\lambda\partial_\lambda))_\mu^\nu=\partial_\mu V^\lambda(\partial_\lambda)^\nu+V^\lambda(\nabla(\partial_\lambda))_\mu^\nu=\partial_\mu V^\mu+V^\lambda\Gamma^\nu_{\mu\lambda}$$We have made the partial derivative covariant by introducing connection coefficients, this object now transforms like a tensor under coordinate transformations. These connection coefficients are a collection of $n^3$ functions on spacetime.

Note these are not the components of a tensor. In another coordinate system, $$\huge\tilde\Gamma^\rho_{\sigma\beta}=(\nabla(\tilde\partial_\beta))_\sigma^\rho$$we assume that the RHS is a $[1,1]$ tensor so that it transforms like a tensor in $\rho,\sigma$. We can rewrite $\tilde\partial_\beta$ in terms of $\partial_\lambda$ by the chain rule:$$\Large\begin{aligned}\tilde\Gamma^\rho_{\sigma\beta}&=\frac{\partial \tilde x^\rho}{\partial x^\nu}\frac{\partial x^\mu}{\partial \tilde x^\sigma}\left(\nabla\left(\frac{\partial x^\lambda}{\partial \tilde x^\beta}\partial_\lambda\right)\right)_\mu^\nu\\
&=\frac{\partial \tilde x^\rho}{\partial x^\nu}\frac{\partial x^\mu}{\partial \tilde x^\sigma}\left(\partial_\mu\left(\frac{\partial x^\lambda}{\partial \tilde x^\beta}\right)\left(\partial_\lambda\right)^\nu+\frac{\partial x^\lambda}{\partial \tilde x^\beta}(\nabla(\partial_\lambda))_\mu^\nu\right)\\
&=\frac{\partial \tilde x^\rho}{\partial x^\nu}\frac{\partial^2 x^\nu}{\partial \tilde x^\sigma\partial\tilde x^\beta}+\frac{\partial \tilde x^\rho}{\partial x^\nu}\frac{\partial x^\mu}{\partial \tilde x^\sigma}\frac{\partial x^\lambda}{\partial \tilde x^\beta}\Gamma^\mu_{\nu\lambda}
\end{aligned}$$The first term prevents this from transforming consistently with the components of a tensor. To choose a connection, we can determine the coefficients in some coordinate system and then determine components in other coordinate systems by solving the above equations.

Given a choice of covariant derivative on vectors, we can define a covariant derivative on other tensors by assuming the same properties. For example, consider the one-form $V^\mu\omega_\mu$:$$\Large\begin{aligned}
\nabla_\nu(V^\mu\omega_\mu)&=\partial_\nu(V^\mu\omega_\mu)\\
&=\partial_\nu V^\mu\omega_\mu+V^\mu\partial_\nu\omega_\mu\\
_\text{Leibniz}\implies&=(\nabla_\nu V^\mu)\omega_\mu+V^\mu\nabla_\nu\omega_\mu\\
\implies V^\mu\nabla_\nu\omega_\mu&=\partial_\nu\ V^\mu\omega_\mu+V^\mu\partial_\nu\omega_\mu-(\nabla_\nu V^\mu)\omega_\mu\\
&=V^\mu\partial_\nu\omega_\mu+(\partial_\nu V^\mu-\nabla_\nu V^\mu)\omega_\mu\\
&=V^\mu\partial_\nu\omega_\mu-\Gamma_{\nu\mu}^\sigma\omega_\sigma\\
\implies\nabla_\nu\omega_\mu&=\partial_\nu\omega_\mu-\Gamma_{\nu\mu}^\sigma\omega_\sigma
\end{aligned}$$Now we have determined the action on both vectors and covectors, we can determine the action on any $[r,s]$ tensor by contracting it with vectors and covectors to form a scalar:$$\Large\nabla_\lambda  T^{\mu_1\dots\mu_r}{}_{\nu_1\dots\nu_s}=\partial_\lambda  T^{\mu_1\dots\mu_r}{}_{\nu_1\dots\nu_s}+\sum_{i=1}^r\Gamma_{\lambda\gamma}^{\mu_i} T^{\mu_1\dots\gamma\dots\mu_r}{}_{\nu_1\dots\nu_s}-\sum_{j=1}^s\Gamma_{\lambda\nu_j}^\beta  T^{\mu_1\dots\mu_r}{}_{\nu_1\dots\beta\dots\nu_s}$$We see that the covariant derivative has a vector-like $+\Gamma$ term for each up index and a covector-like $-\Gamma$ term for each down index.

A connection is torsion-free if the connection coefficients are symmetric in lower indices, $\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu}=\Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\mu}$. Torsion-free connections have the property that covariant derivatives on a scalar commute:$$\Large\begin{aligned}
[\nabla_\mu,\nabla_\nu]f&=\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu f-\nabla_\nu\nabla_\mu f\\
&=(\partial_\mu\partial_\nu f-\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu}f)-(\partial_\nu\partial_\mu f-\Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\mu}f)=0
\end{aligned}$$
