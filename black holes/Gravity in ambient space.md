
A gravitational field is a physical quantity induced by a mass $m$. The gravitational field $\underline{g}$ around a single particle of mass $M$ is a vector field. The magnitude of the field at each point is found by applying the universal gravitational law. One can show that this field is conservative, and so can be expressed using a scalar function $\Phi$, called the gravitational potential.

This allows us to define the gravitational field equation$$\Huge \underline{g}=\frac{d^2\underline{R}}{dt^2}=-GM\frac{\underline{R}}{|\underline{R}|^3}=-\underline{\nabla}\Phi$$where $M$ is the mass of the particle generating the field. ![[Gravity in ambient space 2026-01-27 19.14.37.excalidraw]]
These classical equations are differential equations of motion for a test particle and can be solved to find the motion of said particle. 

The problem with this model of gravity is that spacetime itself is warped under a mass $M$, and so we require a better definition of $\underline{R}$, as this will change with the curvature of spacetime. In general relativity, the gravitational field is determined by solving the Einstein field equations$$\Huge \mathbf{G}=\kappa\mathbf{T}=\frac{8\pi G}{c^4}\mathbf{G}$$where $\mathbf{T}$ is the stress-energy tensor, $\mathbf{G}$ is the Einstein tensor, and $\kappa$ is the Einstein gravitational constant. 

These field equations relate the geometry of spacetime to the distribution of matter within it. The local spacetime curvature is expressed in the Einstein tensor, while the local energy, momentum, and stress are expressed within the stress-energy tensor.
