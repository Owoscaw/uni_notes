
We now move on to study linear, second order partial differential equations, in particular the Poisson, Heat, and Wave equations. These are not only important in their own right, but are representative examples of [[Introduction to PDEs#Classification of PDEs|elliptic, parabolic, and hyperbolic]] PDEs. Many results from the equations we study can be extended to these broader classes of PDEs.

Previously, we studied the general method for solving first order PDEs, the [[The Method of Characteristics|method of characteristics]]. There is no such method for second order PDEs, and we must use different methods for the different PDE classifications. 

In some special cases, where the PDE domain has a particular geometry, we can find explicit solutions using:
> Separation of variables (Laplace's equation on a disc/square)
> [[Fourier Series]] (periodic domains)
> [[Fourier transform]] ($\Re^n$)

We will give explicit solutions for all of the equations mentioned, in all of $\Re^n$. For general domains $\Omega\subset\Re^n$ it is not possible to find explicit solutions, instead we study well-posedness and properties of these solutions.
