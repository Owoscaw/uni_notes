
We will now consider a special type of [[Introduction to PDEs#Quasi-linear PDEs|quasi-linear]] first order PDEs that describe conserved quantities such as mass and charge. The general form for scalar conservation laws for functions is:$$\Huge\partial_tu(\underline{x},t)+\text{div}_{\underline{x}}\underline{f}(u(\underline{x},t))=0$$where $u:\Re^n\times[0,\infty)\rightarrow\Re$ and $\underline{f}:\Re\rightarrow\Re^n$. Here, $\underline{f}$ is known as the flux function. Note that:$$\Huge\begin{align*}
\text{div}_\underline{x}\underline{f}(u(\underline{x},t))&=\sum_{j=1}^n\frac{\partial }{\partial x_j}f_j(u(\underline{x},t))\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^n \frac{df_i}{du}(u(\underline{x},t))\partial_{x_j}u(\underline{x},t)\\
&=\underline{f}'(u(\underline{x},t))\cdot\underline{\nabla}_{\underline{x}}u(\underline{x},t)
\end{align*}$$More generally, systems of conservation laws for vector-valued functions $\underline{u}=\underline{u}(\underline{x},t)$ have the form:$$\Huge \partial_t\underline{u}(\underline{x},t)+\text{div}_{\underline{x}}\underline{F}(\underline{u}(\underline{x},t))=0$$where $\underline{u}:\Re^n\times[0,\infty)\rightarrow\Re^m$ and $\underline{F}:\Re^m\rightarrow\Re^{m\times n}$ is a matrix-valued function with components $\underline{F}(\underline{u})_{ij}=f_{ij}(\underline{u})$ for $i\in\{1,\dots,m\},j\in\{1,\dots,n\}$. The divergence term is a vector and its $i$-th component is the divergence of the $i$-th row of $\underline{F}(\underline{u}(\underline{x,t}))$:$$\large(\text{div}_\underline{x}\underline{F}(\underline{u}(\underline{x},t)))_i=\sum_{j=1}^n\frac{\partial }{\partial x_j}f_{ij}(\underline{u}(\underline{x},t))=\sum_{j=1}^n\sum_{k=1}^m\frac{\partial f_{ij}}{\partial u_k}(\underline{u}(\underline{x},t))\partial_{x_k}u_k(\underline{x},t)$$

Take for example Euler's continuity equation:$$\Huge\begin{cases}\partial_tu(\underline{x},t)+\text{div}_\underline{x}(\underline{\beta}(\underline{x})u(\underline{x},t))=0&x\in\Omega \\
u(\underline{x},0)=u_0(\underline{x})&x\in\partial\Omega\end{cases}$$with the no flux condition $\underline{\beta}(\underline{x})\cdot\underline{\hat{n}}(\underline{x})=0$ where $\underline{\hat{n}}$ is the unit normal to $\partial\Omega$. We claim that if $u,\beta,\Omega$ are smooth then:$$\Huge\int_\Omega u(\underline{x},t)d\underline{x}=\int_\Omega u_0(\underline{x})d\underline{x}$$It is enough to show that $\partial_t\int_\Omega u(\underline{x},t)d\underline{x}=0$ as:$$\Huge\begin{align*}
\partial_t\int_\Omega u(\underline{x},t)d\underline{x}&=\int_\Omega\partial_tu(\underline{x},t)d\underline{x}\\
&=\int_\Omega\text{div}_\underline{x}(\underline{\beta}(\underline{x})u(\underline{x},t))d\underline{x}\\
&=\int_{\partial\Omega}\underline{\beta}(\underline{x})u(\underline{x},t)\cdot\underline{\hat{n}}
(\underline{x})d\underline{x}=0\end{align*}$$

Consider cars on a straight single lane, parametrised by $x\in\Re$. We denote $u(x,t)$ as the density of cars at point $x$ and time $t$. We denote $\phi(x,t)$ the flux of cars at point $x$ and time $t$, that is the ratio at which cars pass point $x$ at time $t$. As no cars can be created or destroyed, for any $-\infty<a<b<\infty$ and $0<t_1<t_2<\infty$ we have:$$\Huge\int_a^bu(x,t_2)dx+\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\phi(a,t)-\phi(b,t)dt=\int_a^bu(x,t_1)dx$$That is to say, the number of cars on $[a,b]$ at time $t_2$ plus the number of cars that entered at $x=a$ minus the number of cars that left at $x=b$ between times $t_1,t_2$ is equal to the number of cars on $[a,b]$ at $t_1$. We can write this as:$$\Huge\int_a^bu(x,t_2)-u(x,t_1)dx=-\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\phi(b,t)-\phi(a,t)dt$$If $u,\phi\in C^1$ we can write the above as:$$\Huge \int_a^b\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\partial_x(x,t)dt\,dx=-\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\int_a^b\partial_x\phi(x,t)dx\,dt$$Then by Fubini's theorem we can change the order of integration:$$\Huge\iint_{[a,b]\times[t_1,t_2]}\partial_tu(x,t)+\partial_x\phi(x,t)\,dx\,dt=0$$
