
Let $\underline{\alpha}:I\rightarrow\Re^3$ be a smooth [[Curves and Surfaces#Curves and curvature|unit speed space curve]] and $\underline{t}(s)=\underline{\alpha}'(s)$ be its unit tangent vector. The curvature of $\underline{\alpha}$ at $\underline{\alpha}(s)$ is then defined as:$$\Huge \kappa(s)=||\underline{t}'(s)||=||\underline{\alpha}''(s)||$$Note that any plane curve $\underline{\alpha}:I\rightarrow\Re^2$ can be viewed as a curve in Euclidean $\Re^3$ with $z$-coordinate constant and equal to $0$.

It is natural to ask about the relation between the curvature function $\kappa_{\underline{\alpha}}$ of the plane curve $\underline{\alpha}$ and the curvature function $\kappa_{\tilde{\underline{\alpha}}}$ of the space curve $\tilde{\underline{\alpha}}$. We actually have $\kappa_{\tilde{\underline{\alpha}}}=|\kappa_{\underline{\alpha}}|$. In contrast to the plane, there is no natural way to rotate $\underline{t}(s)\in\Re^3$ by $\pi/2$ to obtain its unit normal vector $\underline{n}(s)$. Differentiating $\underline{t}(s)\cdot\underline{t}(s)=1$ gives:$$\Huge\underline{t}'(s)\cdot\underline{t}(s)=0$$That is, $\underline{t}'(s)$ is perpendicular to $\underline{t}(s)$. Recall that the length of $\underline{t}'(s)$ is by definition the curvature, and if $\kappa(s)\neq0$ we can normalise $\underline{t}'(s)$ to obtain the unit normal:$$\Huge \underline{n}(s)=\frac{\underline{t}'(s)}{||\underline{t}'(s)||}=\frac{\underline{t}'(s)}{\kappa(s)}$$This unit normal is sometimes called the principal normal vector. We still aim to find a third vector $\underline{b}(s)\in\Re^3$ to obtain an orthonormal basis $\underline{t}(s),\underline{n}(s),\underline{b}(s)\in\Re^3$ adapted to the space curve $\underline{\alpha}$ that moves along the curve under the changing arc length parameter $s\in I$. A basis is called positively oriented if:$$\Huge\det\begin{vmatrix}a_1 & a_2 & a_3 \\ b_1 & b_2 & b_3 \\ c_1 & c_2 & c_3\end{vmatrix}>0$$for basis vectors $\underline{a},\underline{b},\underline{c}\in\Re^3$.

Let $\underline{\alpha}:I\rightarrow\Re^3$ be a smooth unit speed space curve and $\underline{t}(s)=\underline{\alpha}'(s)$ be its unit tangent vector and $\kappa:I\rightarrow\Re$ be its curvature function. If $\kappa(s)\neq0$ then the principal normal vector exists and we define:$$\Huge \underline{b}(s)=\underline{t}(s)\times\underline{n}(s)$$This vector is known as the binormal vector of $\underline{\alpha}$ at $\underline{\alpha}(s)$. We then call the oriented orthonormal basis:$$\Huge \underline{t}(s),\underline{n}(s),\underline{b}(s)\in\Re^3$$the moving frame of $\underline{\alpha}$ at $\underline{\alpha}(s)$.

As this is an orthonormal basis, we can differentiate $\underline{b}(s)\cdot\underline{t}(s)=0$ to get:$$(\underline{b}(s)\cdot\underline{t}(s))'=\underline{b}'(s)\cdot\underline{t}(s)+\underline{b}(s)\cdot\underline{t}'(s)=\underline{b}'(s)\cdot\underline{t}(s)+\underline{b}(s)\cdot(\kappa(s)\underline{n}(s))=\underline{b}'(s)\cdot\underline{t}(s)=0$$That is, $\underline{b}'(s)$ is perpendicular to $\underline{t}(s)$ and therefore parallel to $\underline{n}(s)$. The proportionality factor between $\underline{b}'(s)$ and $\underline{n}(s)$ is called the torsion:$$\Huge \underline{b}'(s)=\tau(s)\underline{n}(s)$$where $\tau(s)$ is the torsion of $\underline{\alpha}$ at $\underline{\alpha}(s)$. Note that this only exists when $\kappa(s)\neq0$, otherwise the principal normal vector is not defined (and therefore no binormal).

# Serret-Frenet equations:

Let $\underline{\alpha}:I\rightarrow\Re^3$ be a smooth unit speed space curve, $\kappa$ and $\tau$ be its curvature and torsion functions respectively. Also let $\underline{t},\underline{n},\underline{b}$ be the associated moving frame, assume $\kappa(s)\neq0$ and we get the following relations:$$\Huge\begin{align*}
\underline{t}'(s)&=\kappa(s)\underline{n}(s)\\
\underline{n}'(s)&=-\kappa(s)\underline{t}(s)-\tau(s)\underline{b}(s)\\
\underline{b}'(s)&=\tau(s)\underline{n}(s)
\end{align*}$$These are known as the Serret-Frenet formulae, and can also be written as:$$\Huge\begin{pmatrix}\underline{t}'(s) \\ \underline{n}'(s) \\ \underline{b}'(s)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0 & \kappa(s) & 0 \\ -\kappa(s) & 0 & -\tau(s) \\ 0 & \tau(s) & 0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\underline{t}(s) \\ \underline{n}(s) \\ \underline{b}(s)\end{pmatrix}$$
We should also have a definition of curvature and torsion for non unit speed curves that do not change under orientation preserving reparametrisations. If $\underline{\alpha}:I\rightarrow\Re^3$ is a smooth regular space curve and $\underline{\beta}=\alpha\circ h:J\rightarrow\Re^3$ is an orientation preserving unit speed reparametrisation with curvature $\kappa_\underline{\beta}$ and torsion $\tau_\underline{\beta}$ we define curvature $\kappa_\underline{\alpha}$ and torsion $\tau_\underline{\alpha}$ as follows:$$\Huge \kappa_\underline{\alpha}(u)=\kappa_\underline{\beta}(h(u)),\,\,\tau_\underline{a}(u)=\tau_\underline{\beta}(h(u))$$for $\kappa_\underline{\beta}(h(u))\neq0$. We can find explicit formulae for these via the following proposition:
> Let $\underline{\alpha}:I\rightarrow\Re^3$ be a smooth regular space curve and $\kappa,\tau$ be its curvature and torsion functions respectively. Assume that $\underline{\alpha}'(u)\times\underline{\alpha}''(u)\neq0$ for some $u\in I$. Then we have $\kappa(u)\neq0$ and that $\tau(u)$ exists, and are given by:$$\Huge \kappa(u)=\frac{||\underline{\alpha}'\times\underline{\alpha}''||}{||\underline{\alpha}'||^3},\,\,\tau(u)=-\frac{(\underline{\alpha}'\times\underline{\alpha}'')\cdot\underline{\alpha}'''}{||\underline{\alpha}'\times\underline{\alpha}''||^2}$$

Take for example the helix given by $\underline{\alpha}(u)=(a\cos u,a\sin u,u)$ for $a>0,u\in\Re$. We can compute:$$\Huge\begin{align*}
\underline{\alpha}'(u)&=(-a\sin u,a\cos u,1)\\
\underline{\alpha}''(u)&=(-a\cos u,-a\sin u,0)\\
\underline{\alpha}'(u)\times\underline{\alpha}''(u)&=(a\sin u,-a\cos u,a^2)\\
\underline{\alpha}'''(u)&=(a\sin u,-a\cos u,0)
\end{align*}$$Which implies $||\underline{\alpha}'(u)||=\sqrt{1+a^2}$ and $||\underline{\alpha}'(u)\times\underline{\alpha}''(u)||=\sqrt{a^2+a^4}=a\sqrt{1+a^2}$, which makes the curvature:$$\Huge\kappa(u)=\frac{||\underline{\alpha}'(u)\times\underline{\alpha}''(u)||}{||\underline{\alpha}'(u)||^3}=\frac{a\sqrt{1+a^2}}{(1+a^2)^{3/2}}=\frac{a}{1+a^2}$$Moreover we have $(\underline{\alpha}'(u)\times\underline{\alpha}''(u))\cdot\underline{\alpha}'''(u)=a^2$, which makes the torsion:$$\Huge\tau(u)=-\frac{(\underline{\alpha}'(u)\times\underline{\alpha}''(u))\cdot\underline{\alpha}'''(u)}{||\underline{\alpha}'(u)\times\underline{\alpha}''(u)||^2}=-\frac{a^2}{(a\sqrt{1+a^2})^2}=-\frac{1}{1+a^2}$$
